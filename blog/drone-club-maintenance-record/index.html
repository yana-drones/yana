Maintain a useful condition and change log for club drones

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

In this guide

  1. Record the observed condition
  2. Document changes completely
  3. Avoid invented schedules and certainty
  4. Example: an unresolved configuration difference
  5. Review the log during handover
  6. Practical activity
  7. Questions
  8. References

Record what was observed, what changed, who reviewed it and which configuration resulted. Keep faults, uncertainties and restrictions visible. A maintenance log supports traceability and competent review; it should not become a collection of unsupported ready-to-fly labels or generic service intervals copied from a different aircraft.

Record the observed condition

Describe visible damage, missing information or a reported symptom precisely. Separate observation from diagnosis. A software warning or unfamiliar sound does not by itself establish the failed component. Include date, equipment identifier and the linked session record. Where photographs help, preserve their context and avoid replacing the original observation with an edited illustration.

Document changes completely

For an approved physical or software change, record the previous and resulting configuration, rationale, reviewer and supporting documents. Note any follow-up required before access resumes. A short entry such as fixed is difficult for future students to evaluate. The record should explain what actually changed and which checks were performed, with their defined limits.

Avoid invented schedules and certainty

Use manufacturer information and the responsible competent assessment for applicable care and review arrangements. Do not assign universal replacement intervals or claim a component’s remaining service life from appearance alone. YANA’s warranty and repair terms require written confirmation; a club log cannot create supplier coverage or turn an unverified repair into an approved operational state.

Example: an unresolved configuration difference

A team notices that the aircraft no longer matches the stored diagram. The log records the difference, restricts affected tasks pending review and identifies the documents needed. The coordinator investigates who made the change and whether it was approved. The purpose is to restore a trustworthy configuration record, not to blame a member or reconnect parts merely to match an old picture.

Review the log during handover

Before new coordinators take over, ask them to explain the current state and open issues from the records alone. ArduPilot’s setup and firmware documentation provides context for configuration-dependent behaviour; the log must still describe the actual shared equipment. Preserve historical entries so a later question can be traced back to the relevant change instead of disappearing under a rewritten summary.

Use a separate current-status summary that links to the historical entries rather than replacing them. This gives everyday users a readable starting point while preserving the reasoning, evidence and unresolved conditions behind the most recent recorded state of the equipment.

  • Separate observations and diagnosis.
  • Record previous and resulting configurations.
  • Name reviewers and unresolved restrictions.
  • Preserve history and supplier terms accurately.

Improve one log entry

  1. Choose an entry that says only checked or fixed.
  2. Add the observation, action and evidence available.
  3. Mark missing details as unresolved rather than reconstructing them from memory.

Questions

Can a log replace competent inspection?

No. It records evidence and decisions but does not independently establish suitability or operational readiness.

Should old entries be deleted after repair?

Retain relevant history with clear status updates. Past changes can explain later questions and support a more accurate handover.
