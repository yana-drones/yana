Connect drone activities to an existing curriculum

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

  2. Select an appropriate artefact
  3. Use external resources as background

  8. References

Begin with the existing course outcome, then select a drone-related task that produces relevant evidence. Record the match and its limits rather than claiming that any hands-on activity automatically meets the curriculum. Ask the responsible teacher or department to approve the mapping and the practical arrangements.

Read the actual course outcome

A lesson can connect to mechanics, electronics, computing or communication, but the relationship must be specific. Identify the action the curriculum expects and the level of explanation required. A component-identification activity may suit an introductory systems goal but provide little evidence for an advanced control-theory outcome. Do not use the presence of a drone as the justification by itself.

Select an appropriate artefact

Choose what learners will produce: a free-body sketch, system map, reviewed interface table or documented data analysis. The artefact should make the relevant course knowledge visible. If equipment access is limited, consider a narrower paper or simulation task and state its limits. Avoid assigning practical competence when learners have only watched an instructor demonstrate it.

Use external resources as background

The ATL curriculum provides a drone module among its educational resources. It can inform discussion, but referring to it does not make YANA an ATL partner or establish formal approval of a workshop. Likewise, engineering-education frameworks can support planning without certifying a course. Keep affiliation and accreditation claims out of materials unless independently substantiated.

Example: map a computing outcome

If a course expects students to document a data-processing method, a labelled sample drone log can provide context. Learners describe fields, transformations and limitations, then reproduce a small chart. The task is assessed for data reasoning rather than aircraft operation. The teacher should confirm the dataset and criteria meet the course requirement before it becomes an assessed activity.

Review the mapping after delivery

Compare learner outputs with the intended outcome and identify gaps. Perhaps the equipment was interesting but the explanation task was too shallow, or the terminology overwhelmed beginners. Revise the activity rather than simply adding more content. Cornell’s outcome-design guidance is useful background for this review. Keep the final curriculum decision with the institution and document any agreed adaptations.

  • Quote the relevant institutional outcome accurately.
  • Select an artefact that demonstrates it.
  • State the limits of the activity’s evidence.
  • Obtain the responsible teacher’s review.

Build a curriculum mapping row

  1. Write one course outcome and one drone-related task.
  2. Name the assessed evidence and its limitations.
  3. Ask the course lead whether the match is sufficient or needs revision.

Questions

Does using the ATL module imply affiliation?

No. It is an educational reference, not evidence of partnership, endorsement or approval for YANA.

Can one workshop cover multiple subjects?

It can connect concepts, but each claimed outcome still needs an appropriate task and evidence. Keep the mapping realistic.
