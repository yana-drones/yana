Read voltage, current and consumed charge from a drone power monitor

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

In this guide

  1. Draw the measurement boundary
  2. Match readings to units and validity
  3. Calculate consumed charge with stated timing
  4. Power is different from charge
  5. Do not turn consumption into a certainty about remaining energy
  6. Practical activity
  7. Questions
  8. References

A power monitor may provide voltage, current or other information depending on its hardware and configuration. Measurement location determines which consumers are included. Integrating current over time estimates consumed charge under assumptions, while voltage multiplied by current gives power. Neither calculation alone proves remaining capacity or flight endurance.

Draw the measurement boundary

Put a fictional sensor on a supply diagram and identify which branches pass through it. A current sensor measuring only an accessory branch cannot describe the entire aircraft. ArduPilot’s power-monitor documentation distinguishes measurement and supply functions. Verify what the actual module measures instead of assuming that any item called a power module provides every useful quantity.

Match readings to units and validity

Voltage and current fields need documented scaling, calibration and unavailable-value conventions. A dashboard can show a plausible number even when its configuration is wrong. For a classroom exercise, provide explicit definitions and one invalid sample. Ask students to keep invalid data distinct from a real zero-current interval rather than smoothing both into the same line.

Calculate consumed charge with stated timing

For a fictional constant current of two amperes over thirty seconds, the simple charge calculation is two multiplied by thirty divided by 3,600, or about 0.0167 amp-hour. That is about 16.7 milliamp-hours. The example is arithmetic only. Variable current requires accounting for the intervals and measurement assumptions rather than multiplying one convenient sample by the whole session duration.

Power is different from charge

A current measurement alone does not establish energy use when voltage matters. Under an illustrative constant ten-volt condition, the two-ampere load uses twenty watts, and thirty seconds corresponds to about 0.167 watt-hour. Keep the voltage assumption visible. Actual monitoring may involve filtering, estimation and calibration that a simple table omits.

Do not turn consumption into a certainty about remaining energy

Consumed charge depends on measurement quality and the chosen starting condition. A remaining-capacity estimate additionally needs information about the real battery and estimator. A class report should identify where each number came from and what remains unknown. Real battery handling and electrical tests stay within manufacturer instructions and qualified supervision; printed data is sufficient for this lesson.

  • Mark which branches the monitor observes.
  • Use documented units and invalid values.
  • Include elapsed time in integration.
  • Separate measured consumption from estimated remaining capacity.

Integrate a fictional current recording

  1. Use three ten-second intervals carrying a fictional constant two-ampere current and ten-volt supply.
  2. Calculate charge in amp-hours and energy in watt-hours, retaining units at every step.
  3. Remove the current sample from one interval and ask how the missing data affects the conclusion.
  4. Move the drawn sensor onto one accessory branch and identify which whole-system claims are no longer supported.

Questions

Does every power module measure current?

No. Capabilities vary. Check the exact module and configuration, including what quantity is measured and where.

Is integrated current a verified remaining-battery percentage?

No. It estimates consumed charge under assumptions. Remaining capacity requires battery and estimator context beyond that calculation.
