Build a drone power budget without guessing flight time

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

A power budget lists electrical consumers, their supply rails and demand under stated conditions. It should distinguish propulsion from lower-power electronics and include conversion losses where evidence supports them. The worksheet identifies missing data and incompatible assumptions; it does not establish actual endurance or replace a verified electrical design.

List consumers by rail and operating condition

A controller, receiver, sensor and motor system may use different supply arrangements. Put the rail voltage, current or power evidence and condition in separate columns. Avoid adding currents from different voltages as though they were directly comparable input demand. A classroom drawing can show several rails branching from a source without prescribing actual aircraft wiring.

Convert demand to a common quantity

For a constant illustrative load, electrical power equals voltage multiplied by current. A fictional five-volt device drawing 0.2 ampere uses one watt at its own input. Another fictional ten-volt device drawing 0.2 ampere uses two watts. The matching current numbers do not imply matching power, which is why a budget needs both unit and rail context.

Conversion introduces another boundary

The input power needed by a regulator is not necessarily the same as its output power. Use an explicitly hypothetical efficiency of 80 percent to show that a one-watt output would require 1.25 watts of input in that model. This is a classroom assumption, not a regulator specification. Real values depend on the actual device and operating conditions.

Monitoring and distribution perform different jobs

PX4’s power-module documentation distinguishes regulated controller supply, voltage-current information and distribution functions. A device may combine jobs, but do not assume every board does. Identify what is measured and where it is measured in the diagram. A current reading from one branch may omit other consumers, making it unsuitable as total-system consumption without further evidence.

Use the budget as an uncertainty map

Finish with unknowns such as actual operating demand, regulator limits and measured propulsion power. Each missing value should have a source or proposed verification method, reviewed by an instructor. No live high-current experiment is required for the introductory lesson. A more complete spreadsheet improves questions, but only suitable actual evidence can support a published aircraft performance claim.

  • Keep rails and measurement locations explicit.
  • Convert compatible quantities to watts.
  • State loss assumptions rather than hiding them.
  • Avoid calculating advertised endurance from a rough budget.

Find the missing branch in a power budget

  1. Draw a fictional source feeding a regulator and a separate propulsion branch.
  2. Calculate one-watt and two-watt accessory loads from the example and include the illustrative conversion loss.
  3. Place a fictional current sensor on only the regulator branch and ask what it fails to observe.
  4. Create an unknowns column identifying the evidence needed before this could become a full-system budget.

Questions

Can currents from different supply voltages be added directly?

Not as a meaningful source-power budget. Account for voltage, conversion paths and measurement boundaries; use compatible power quantities where appropriate.

Does a complete-looking budget prove flight time?

No. Actual usable energy, operating demand, aircraft configuration and conditions need verified evidence. A planning worksheet is not a flight test.
