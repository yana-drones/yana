Turn a one-off drone workshop into continued learning

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

In this guide

  2. Sort unanswered questions
  3. Choose a small continuation

  6. Practical activity
  7. Questions
  8. References

Collect the diagrams, questions and inspection notes produced during the workshop, then choose one manageable follow-up task. Share resources matched to the actual equipment and assign an institutional owner. Continued learning depends on access, supervision and a clear next question, not simply a longer reading list.

Preserve the work learners made

Ask teams to photograph or scan their annotated diagrams and organise them by task. Record corrections from the facilitator so an early misconception does not become the permanent reference. Keep personal information out of shared folders unless needed and authorised. A small set of well-labelled outputs is easier for the next coordinator to reuse than a large unsorted image collection.

Sort unanswered questions

Separate conceptual questions, documentation gaps and practical changes to equipment. A question about an ESC’s role may need a short explanation; uncertainty about a connector needs manufacturer documentation. A proposed hardware modification needs review before action. This triage helps learners continue productively without interpreting every unanswered question as permission to experiment on the shared aircraft.

Choose a small continuation

Set a task that can succeed with the resources the institution actually retains. It might be a corrected system diagram, a component comparison or an analysis of a labelled sample log. Do not assign assembly if the equipment was only loaned for the event. Agree who will supervise access and who can answer technical questions before describing follow-up support publicly.

Example: review the same diagram twice

Immediately after a workshop, ask learners to explain the power and signal paths on their diagram. After a later reading task, ask them to revise one uncertain connection and cite the document used. Compare the explanations rather than measuring success by how many links were opened. This makes progress visible without requiring a new flying demonstration or additional hardware purchase.

Evaluate the event honestly

Ask what learners can explain now, which activities were difficult and what resources would support the next step. A satisfaction score alone does not show technical learning. Cornell distinguishes group outcomes and individual contributions when evaluating teamwork; use that distinction to avoid assuming one confident presenter represents everyone. Any future YANA support must be included in the agreed scope rather than inferred from workshop attendance.

  • Store corrected outputs and source references.
  • Classify questions before assigning actions.
  • Check retained equipment and supervised access.
  • Choose one next task and a review date.

Create a follow-up action sheet

  1. Select three unresolved questions from the workshop.
  2. Assign one owner and a suitable evidence source for each.
  3. Review the answers before approving any physical changes.

Questions

Should follow-up always involve more building?

No. Documentation, explanation and data analysis can address important gaps before another physical task is appropriate.

Is ongoing provider support automatically included?

No. Ask what follow-up access, response arrangements and duration are included in the written workshop proposal.
