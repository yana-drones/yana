Battery capacity and energy: understand mAh, Ah and Wh

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

Amp-hours describe electric charge capacity, while watt-hours describe energy. For a simple nominal comparison, multiply nominal voltage by capacity in amp-hours. Equal mAh values at different voltages do not imply equal energy. These label calculations help comparison, but they do not establish usable energy or flight duration for a real aircraft.

Convert the label before calculating

A capacity of 2,000 milliamp-hours equals 2 amp-hours. The milli prefix represents one thousandth, so divide by 1,000 before multiplying by volts. Write the conversion explicitly in a worksheet. Omitting that step creates a thousandfold numerical error that can look plausible if the final result has no unit or comparison check.

Compare two fictional packs

For arithmetic only, imagine pack A labelled 10 volts nominal and 2 amp-hours, and pack B labelled 15 volts nominal and 2 amp-hours. Their nominal energy calculations are 20 and 30 watt-hours. Neither label describes a YANA package or an actual recommended battery. The example shows why capacity alone is incomplete when comparing different nominal voltages.

Nominal energy is not guaranteed usable energy

A real pack’s delivered energy depends on operating conditions, condition and the allowed limits in its documentation. Not all labelled energy is necessarily available for a particular task. PX4’s battery setup material distinguishes measured information from remaining-capacity estimation. Keep label arithmetic separate from measured consumption and from any operational decision about a real battery.

Power and energy answer different questions

Watts describe a rate of energy transfer; watt-hours describe an amount. A fictional load of five watts for two hours uses ten watt-hours in a constant-power model. A drone does not necessarily have constant power consumption, and adding battery mass can change the required power. Therefore a simple energy division is not a verified flight-time result.

Build a comparison table with missing data visible

Use columns for nominal voltage, capacity, calculated nominal energy, mass and source. Mark unavailable values as unknown rather than estimating them from a photograph. Keep charging, storage and handling decisions within manufacturer guidance and qualified supervision. The learning objective is unit literacy, which can be taught entirely with printed labels and a spreadsheet.

  • Convert mAh to Ah before multiplying.
  • Use nominal voltage for the stated comparison.
  • Separate energy from power.
  • Do not publish a flight-time claim from label arithmetic.

Compare three invented battery labels

  1. Create fictional labels for 10 V/2 Ah, 15 V/2 Ah and 10 V/3 Ah packs, clearly marking them as classroom examples.
  2. Calculate nominal watt-hours and identify which packs have equal nominal energy.
  3. Add a fictional five-watt accessory and calculate its two-hour energy use as a separate exercise.
  4. List the missing evidence required before any of these calculations could describe a real aircraft’s endurance.

Questions

Does a larger mAh number always mean a longer flight?

No. Voltage, mass, usable energy, aircraft power demand and conditions matter. A capacity label alone cannot establish flight duration.

Is the calculated watt-hour value a charging instruction?

No. It is a nominal energy comparison. Follow the actual battery and charger manufacturer instructions for handling and charging.
