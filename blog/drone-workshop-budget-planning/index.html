Build a realistic drone workshop budget without guessed prices

Budget by responsibility and inclusion rather than using an attractive per-student figure in isolation. Ask what is provided, retained, consumed and returned, then separate delivery costs from equipment ownership. Use supplier quotations for amounts and mark unresolved items explicitly instead of filling gaps with guesses.

Define the unit of purchase

A workshop fee might cover a facilitator, a shared set of equipment, individual materials or some combination. Those are different purchases. Start with the learning format and identify what the institution actually receives. If learners share one assembly, state that clearly in internal approvals and public event information. A payment per participant does not necessarily mean a drone or kit per participant.

Create inclusion and exclusion columns

List teaching materials, tools, demonstration equipment, computers, consumables and follow-up support. Assign each to the host, provider or an unresolved category. Record ownership after the event. This avoids buying duplicate items while missing essentials. Do not assume a pictured battery, charger or transmitter is part of a quotation; request explicit confirmation of each item and its suitability.

Account for logistics and changes

Ask about travel, equipment transport, taxes, venue preparation and rescheduling terms. Include a decision date for confirming attendance and scope. If the institution wants an extra practical task, assess its material and supervision implications before treating it as a free addition. A contingency allowance is an internal planning choice, not a substitute for understanding unclear commercial terms.

Example: compare two proposals fairly

Suppose one proposal includes shared equipment returned after teaching, while another includes an institution-owned assembly. Compare the delivery service separately from retained equipment. Ask whether both include the same learner outputs and preparation. The less expensive headline may be a perfectly appropriate option for an introductory event; it simply answers a different ownership question. No standard price is implied by this example.

Link spending to outcomes

For each significant cost line, name the learning task it enables. An additional display might improve visibility; duplicate components might increase individual participation. If the connection is weak, ask whether the item can wait. The ATL procurement resources are relevant background for institutions using that programme, but their rules do not automatically govern every college purchase. Follow your own institution’s applicable procurement process.

  • Separate delivery from retained equipment.
  • Confirm quantities, ownership and exclusions.
  • Request tax, logistics and change terms.
  • Use written quotations for every amount.

Draft a blank comparison budget

  1. Create columns for item, quantity, owner, supplier and confirmed amount.
  2. Add an unresolved-items section rather than estimating silently.
  3. Ask the approving office which documents it requires.

Questions

Does YANA publish a fixed workshop price?

Pricing depends on the agreed scope and must be confirmed in a written proposal. Do not infer a price from this planning guide.

Should the cheapest offer be chosen?

Compare learning scope, equipment ownership, support and responsibilities first. Price becomes meaningful when the offers describe comparable work.
