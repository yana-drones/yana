What should an educational drone quotation include?

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

In this guide

  1. Identify the supplied assembly
  2. List accessories explicitly
  3. Include documentation and software context
  4. Example: the missing charger question
  5. Confirm services and exclusions
  6. Practical activity
  7. Questions
  8. References

Ask for a written list of every supplied item, its quantity and status, including accessories, documents and services. Separate required but excluded items from optional additions. Photographs and general product names do not establish package contents. Confirm compatibility, ownership and acceptance arrangements before approving the purchase.

Identify the supplied assembly

The quote should distinguish a frame kit, partly assembled system and completed educational assembly. Request identifiers for the proposed controller and other agreed components. Ask whether the delivered arrangement will match the photographs. YANA’s published F450/Pixhawk 2.4.8 configuration remains proposed; the itemised final configuration must be agreed rather than inferred from an image or a similar online listing.

List accessories explicitly

Ask separately about the receiver, transmitter, battery, charger, cables, mounting parts and tools that the agreed activity requires. This is a checklist of questions, not a statement that every package must contain all those items. Confirm the exact compatible choices and quantities. A component visible during a demonstration may be loaned equipment rather than something the buyer will retain.

Include documentation and software context

Request a configuration record, applicable source references and instructions describing the supported learning tasks. Ask what software environment was used and what the institution must provide. ArduPilot’s setup guidance separates several stages of preparation; a generic link alone may not describe the supplied system. Verify that the handover documents identify which stages have actually been completed and reviewed.

Example: the missing charger question

An institution sees a battery in the product photograph and assumes a charger is supplied. A clear quote instead states whether either item is included, which exact models are proposed and who verifies their suitability. If an item is excluded, the institution can obtain a compatible option through its own process. Resolving the question before purchase is better than discovering it during a workshop.

Confirm services and exclusions

List any training, delivery, demonstration and follow-up support as distinct lines with scope and terms. Ask about taxes, acceptance, changes and unresolved commercial details. Warranty or repair terms must be supplied by the seller rather than assumed from the product category. Retain the final accepted version with the equipment so later users can see what was actually purchased and which responsibilities stayed with the host.

  • State assembly status and component identifiers.
  • List accessories with quantities and compatibility.
  • Identify documents and software context.
  • Separate included services, exclusions and optional items.

Mark up a sample quote

  1. Highlight every pictured item that lacks a written line.
  2. Add a question for every required but unspecified accessory.
  3. Request an updated itemised version before approval.

Questions

Does a photo prove an accessory is included?

No. Ask the written quotation to identify each included item, quantity and ownership arrangement.

Are battery and charger details confirmed for YANA?

They should be treated as unconfirmed until the final configuration and itemised written quotation specify them.
