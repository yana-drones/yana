Drone compass data: heading, interference and evidence

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

In this guide

  2. Treat the sensor environment as part of the system

  7. Questions

A compass supplies magnetic information used in heading estimation, but its readings can be affected by installation and nearby magnetic influences. Heading also differs from direction of travel. Start with clear definitions and a documented sensor installation; do not assume a calibration message eliminates every source of magnetic interference.

Heading is not the travel direction

A paper model can face north while moving east across a grid. Its heading and ground-track direction differ. Give each arrow a separate colour and label. This simple example prevents students from using a position trajectory as automatic proof of the direction the vehicle faced, especially when analysing recordings without a visible front marker.

Treat the sensor environment as part of the system

A magnetic sensor observes its local environment rather than an abstract perfect north arrow. Nearby materials and electrical systems can matter. ArduPilot’s advanced compass guidance discusses installation and sensor selection. The conceptual lesson is to record the sensor’s identity and surroundings, not to derive a universal separation distance from a photograph or generic component family.

Calibration and changing conditions are different questions

A calibration procedure addresses defined error characteristics under its assumptions. It does not mean every future measurement will be unaffected by the environment. Students can use two fictional datasets with a steady offset and a changing disturbance to see why those are different patterns. Actual calibration or interference investigations require the stack’s documented process and qualified supervision.

Use a controlled observation worksheet

For an unpowered classroom compass demonstration, document the starting orientation and surrounding objects before changing one nonhazardous item. Avoid presenting it as a controller acceptance test. If using supplied logs instead, include the equipment configuration and time. A changed heading trace alone cannot distinguish physical rotation, magnetic effects and software interpretation without more evidence.

Report a hypothesis with a next check

A useful conclusion might say the recorded change is consistent with a changed local magnetic environment, then identify what could test that idea. It should not declare a sensor faulty from one plot. Preserve uncertainty and avoid using general compass explanations to justify operational settings. The educational aim is careful interpretation, not a shortcut around system-specific checks.

  • Separate heading from direction of travel.
  • Record sensor identity and local environment.
  • Distinguish steady offset from changing disturbance.
  • Keep calibration claims within their documented scope.

Read heading and motion separately

  1. Move a north-facing paper model east across a grid and draw heading and travel arrows.
  2. Provide a fictional heading recording with a steady offset, then one with a temporary disturbance.
  3. Ask teams for at least two possible explanations for each pattern and an additional observation to distinguish them.
  4. Write a sensor-environment record listing known facts and unanswered installation questions.

Questions

Does a north-facing drone always move north?

No. Heading describes orientation. Motion direction depends on the actual movement, and the two can differ.

Does successful calibration prove there is no interference?

No. Calibration has a defined purpose and assumptions. Installation and changing environmental influences still require appropriate system-specific review.
