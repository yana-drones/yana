Give feedback that improves a student drone project

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

Tie feedback to the project’s agreed question and point to a specific observation or missing piece of evidence. Explain why the issue matters, then suggest a manageable next action. Separate essential corrections from optional ideas, and let learners respond with reasoning rather than treating every reviewer preference as a new requirement.

Begin with the agreed purpose

Before commenting on a diagram or report, read the stated outcome and scope. A suggestion that is interesting but unrelated can distract a team from completing its actual investigation. Identify whether your comment concerns accuracy, evidence, readability or future work. This helps students decide how to act and prevents all feedback from appearing equally urgent.

Describe the observable issue

Replace vague comments such as this is confusing with a precise statement: the diagram does not identify which connector view it uses. Explain the consequence, such as another reader being unable to compare it with the source. Keep criticism directed at the artefact and reasoning. Do not infer carelessness or competence from one unresolved label.

Request a bounded revision

Ask for the smallest change that resolves the issue or exposes the uncertainty honestly. If the required information is unavailable, a labelled unresolved note may be more appropriate than a rushed experiment. Cornell’s peer-assessment guidance encourages constructive, descriptive feedback; the drone-project examples here apply that approach to technical records and group review.

Example: improve an unsupported conclusion

A report says the prototype is reliable after one demonstration. Useful feedback asks what the demonstration actually observed, how reliability is being defined and which evidence supports that wider claim. A bounded revision might replace the statement with a description of the observed event and its conditions. It should not pressure students to invent a reliability figure or run unapproved tests.

Check the response to feedback

Ask learners to record whether they accepted, modified or deferred the suggestion and why. Review the resulting evidence rather than only checking that the wording changed. Preserve optional ideas in a future-work list. This keeps feedback part of learning and project control instead of allowing an expanding comment thread to quietly redefine the assessment.

  • Link feedback to the agreed outcome.
  • Describe a specific issue and its effect.
  • Suggest a bounded evidence-based revision.
  • Separate essential changes from optional ideas.

Rewrite three review comments

  1. Choose a vague comment from a draft review.
  2. Name the exact observation and why it matters.
  3. Add one manageable next action and mark its priority.

Questions

Must students accept every suggestion?

No. They should consider it, explain their response and follow the supervisor’s assessment arrangements. Some suggestions belong outside the current scope.

Is feedback only for the final report?

No. Earlier feedback on a diagram, method or claim gives learners time to improve the evidence before the deadline.
