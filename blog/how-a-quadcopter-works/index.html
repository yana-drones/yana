How a quadcopter works: follow the energy and information

By YANAPublished 27 October 20263 min read

In this guide

  2. Thrust gives the structure a job
  3. A controller repeatedly compares and responds
  4. One failure can affect several paths
  5. Check understanding with an imagined change
  6. Practical activity
  7. Questions
  8. References

A quadcopter uses four motor-driven propellers to produce thrust. Its flight controller compares a requested motion with estimated motion, then adjusts motor commands. Electrical power and control information follow different paths, so a useful first lesson is to trace both rather than memorise a parts list.

Start with two separate maps

Draw the aircraft twice. On the first drawing, connect the battery, distribution hardware, electronic speed controllers and motors with thick lines. On the second, connect the receiver, flight controller, sensors and speed controllers with thin lines. A component can appear on both maps because it may consume energy while exchanging information. Keep the two meanings visually distinct.

Thrust gives the structure a job

The propellers accelerate air, and the resulting aerodynamic force acts on the vehicle. The frame holds the motors at defined positions and transfers their forces through the structure. A propeller is therefore part of an interacting system, not a decorative attachment. For this conceptual lesson use a cardboard rotor drawing; powered demonstrations add risks without helping the initial mapping exercise.

A controller repeatedly compares and responds

Suppose a paper model should remain level but a sensor estimate says its right side is low. A controller needs an appropriate corrective moment, not simply more power everywhere. The exact allocation depends on geometry and firmware. This distinction separates the desired outcome, the estimated state and the output command. Those three quantities should receive different labels on a diagram.

One failure can affect several paths

A loose connector might interrupt a sensor message, its power supply, or both. A dashboard showing no position update does not immediately prove the receiver itself failed. Ask what evidence would distinguish a missing measurement from a failed communication link. Troubleshooting becomes clearer when students can point to a particular path rather than say that the entire drone is broken.

Check understanding with an imagined change

Add a fictional position sensor to the map. Students should identify its mounting, power, data interface and software interpretation separately. An unused connector does not establish electrical or firmware compatibility. The learning outcome is an annotated architecture drawing that another team can read without guessing. Actual component choices, supported firmware and package contents require verification on the specific aircraft.

  • Label energy and information paths differently.
  • Identify the controller, estimator and actuator roles.
  • Record interfaces as unknown when documentation is missing.

Build a component relationship map

  1. Give each group eight paper component cards and two colours of string; leave all physical hardware disconnected.
  2. Ask students to route power in one colour and information in the other, then explain why an ESC belongs on both maps.
  3. Remove one string and ask for two possible observations and one additional observation that would distinguish the causes.
  4. Submit a labelled map and a short explanation of what the exercise cannot prove about a real vehicle.

Questions

Does a flight controller supply the motors with their main power?

Usually the motor power path runs through the battery and ESCs. Controller output signals request actuator behaviour; they are not the main propulsion supply.

Does this diagram describe the final YANA package?

No. It is a general learning model. The F450 frame and Pixhawk 2.4.8 are proposed; other configuration and package details need confirmation.
