Hands-on drone building workshops

Plan hands-on drone-making and drone-development learning for your college, robotics club or makerspace. Professional-led training is scoped with you before booking.

Learn by working through a real system.

A workshop can connect component identification, mechanical assembly, electronics and flight-control concepts. We’ll start with the students’ experience and your institution’s learning objectives.

Ask about professional-led sessions for your group. Facilitator availability, relevant experience and the agreed responsibilities will be shared in the proposal; no specific instructor or credential is advertised here.

A workshop booking does not guarantee that every participant will build or fly a separate drone. Agree the actual activity and equipment allocation first.

For the organiser

  • Share the learners’ age range, prior experience and learning goals.
  • Confirm venue access, tables, power, computers and internet needs.
  • Agree group size, equipment allocation, supervision and an equipment handover plan.
  • Identify the host coordinator and confirm any photography permissions.

For the proposal

  • Named facilitator and relevant professional experience for your review.
  • Session format, duration, materials and practical activities.
  • Responsibilities for equipment, venue, permissions and supervision.
  • Itemised cost, scheduling, deliverables and any follow-up support.

Workshop questions

Can you work with our college or robotics club?

Send your institution, location, preferred dates, group size and goals. Delivery and availability are confirmed after the scope is reviewed.

Is a workshop a pilot certification course?

No pilot certification or regulatory approval is advertised. This is an educational workshop enquiry offer. Any proposed completion document must be described accurately in the quote.

Will a flying demonstration be included?

Only if it is appropriate, permitted and explicitly agreed. A classroom invitation does not establish airspace permission. A non-flying demonstration or simulation may be more suitable.

Can teachers or club mentors join?

Include faculty or mentor development in the brief. Any onboarding, documentation or follow-up sessions must be included in the proposal.
